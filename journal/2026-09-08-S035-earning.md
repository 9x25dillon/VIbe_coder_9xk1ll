## What was built

**Earning and equipping**, in [`abilities.py`](../vibecoder/abilities.py).

```
── ABILITIES ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  your class picks which two; your scores decide when.
  Comprehensionist offers:

    ✔ Refactor     take a spent repair back           cost: the boss recovers 8
    · Overclock    every repair left becomes 4 damage needs 2 tags measured at 75% or better
```

`earned()` is a **set intersection** — `unlocked_by(class) & gates_met(mastery)`
— and there is no arithmetic combination anywhere on the path. That is exit
criterion 8 held at the one seam that could plausibly break it: **no blended
number exists to display, because none is computed.**

Three properties make it a gate rather than a score, and each has a test:

- **Every class offers exactly two.** W8's "no class outranks another",
  extended to what they unlock. An Architect is not short of a Refactor; they
  have a different two.
- **Mastery never changes *which*** a class offers, and **the class never
  changes what a gate requires.** Scoring more cannot hand you another class's
  abilities; rewriting your code cannot lower a bar.
- **A gate counts confident tags at or above a level, never an average.** An
  average would let a player unlock something by being adequate at everything,
  which is a different claim from having got good at some of it — and would
  quietly reintroduce the single blended figure on one side of the wall.

A player with no class gets nothing, framed as an invitation rather than a
penalty: abilities are flavoured by how you write, and that is simply not
known until they profile something.

## Misconceptions and corrections

### M57 — I reported a criterion as unmeetable when the instrument check was

**Believed, and written into three documents:** that T4's exit criterion 3 was
partly unmeetable — that the 60–90% success band "cannot hold for a player who
always writes correct code", and that this was a finding against the
criterion. I put it in S028's journal, its data twin, the flight board, T4
itself, and told the user it was one of two things standing between T4 and a
clean landing.

**Revealed by:** Reading criterion 3 and the instrument check side by side
before landing, because landing is the moment the comparison is forced (M45's
lesson, and this is the first time I applied it deliberately rather than
learning it again).

The document draws the distinction itself, in its own words:

> **Criterion 3.** Success rate across **a** simulated 50-level run stays
> inside 60–90%.
>
> **Instrument check.** Simulated players (always-correct, always-naive,
> improving, plateaued) run against the policy; assert the success band holds
> **for each**.

The criterion asks about *a* run. That is verified and robust: 72% for an
improving player across 40/40 seeds, 71% for a plateaued one across 38/40. The
**instrument check** is the thing demanding all four, and it is that which
cannot be satisfied — the finding is real, it is simply against a different
line.

**Corrected to:** criterion 3 verified; the instrument check recorded as
partly unmeetable in T4's table, alongside the two that are met. Neither is
re-cut (N7). Q89 still carries the substance.

**Cost:** none in code. It cost a false claim standing in four places for a
few hours, and it would have cost T4 landing on seven criteria out of eight —
or not landing at all — for no reason.

**Generalisable:** I was reading my own summary rather than the source, which
is exactly M45 with the roles reversed. There, a board summary hid an unmet
criterion; here, a summary I wrote invented an unmeetable one. **A summary
drifts in whichever direction the summariser was worried about** — I had just
spent a session on the power-creep hazard and read the next line pessimistically.
The fix is the same either way and it is cheap: when a criterion's status
matters, quote it.
