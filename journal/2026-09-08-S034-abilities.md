## What was built

**Three abilities**, in [`abilities.py`](../vibecoder/abilities.py), each
priced in the fight's own currency so every one is a trade rather than a gift:

| Ability | Does | Costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Refactor** | takes a spent repair back | the boss recovers 8 |
| **Steady Hand** | the next repair heals the boss nothing | one repair from the pool |
| **Overclock** | every repair left becomes 4 damage | every repair; you cannot repair again |

`fight.py` gained the four mutators these need — `refund`, `concede`,
`waive_next_heal`, `bank_repairs` — so `abilities.py` never reaches into its
private fields. Every one clamps to what the fight can actually afford, which
is why `use` reports the price *paid* rather than the price asked: an ability
that announced a cost the fight could not meet would be lying on screen.

### The one that threatens the engine

T4's hazard is specific: *power creep dissolves the boss engine.* Working out
which ability was dangerous took thinking about how a fight is actually lost.

**A fight is lost by failing a step with no repairs left.** Hit points do not
enter into it — a boss at full health is a poor win, not a loss. So the
dangerous ability is not one that damages the boss. It is the one that *adds
repairs*, and `Refactor` is exactly that.

The bound is to price it in **damage already dealt**: you may buy attempts
only with progress you have already made. Two consequences fall out, and both
are the ones needed:

- a player failing the *first* step has dealt nothing and can buy nothing —
  precisely the player who would otherwise never lose;
- every purchase moves the boss back toward full, and once it is there the
  supply is gone.

### Breaking the guard on purpose

`TestAFightIsStillLosable` is an attempt rather than an assertion: a player
who cannot solve the last step plays every ability as well as it can be
played, and the question is whether the fight ever ends.

A guard that cannot fail is not a guard (M46), so I removed `Refactor`'s cost
and ran it. **Seven tests failed, including all four losability ones.**
Restored, all 22 pass. That is the difference between a test that guards
criterion 7 and a test that restates it.

The measured cost of survival is worth keeping: by the time an
abilities-equipped player finally runs out, **the boss is back at full
health** — every point of progress sold back for attempts.

## Misconceptions and corrections

### M56 — I nearly bounded the wrong resource

**Believed, while designing:** That the way to keep abilities from dissolving
the boss engine was to make them *cost hit points* — the boss heals, so
abilities are self-limiting.

**Revealed by:** Writing down how a fight is actually lost before writing the
abilities, which the previous handoff told me to do. **Hit points cannot end a
fight.** `cmd_boss` returns 1 when a step fails and `fight.can_repair` is
false; the boss's health is never consulted. A boss at full health is a poor
win. So an ability priced purely in HP is priced in a currency the player can
spend without limit — healing a boss that is already full costs nothing at
all, and `Fight.remaining` clamps at `hp` precisely so the bar cannot read
104.

An ability costing "the boss heals 8" would therefore have been **free after
the boss returned to full**, and would have handed out unlimited repairs to
whoever needed them most.

**Corrected to:** `concede` is capped at `dealt - healed` — what you have
actually taken and not yet given back — and `Refactor.available` requires that
much to exist. The cost is *progress*, which is finite, rather than *the
boss's health*, which is not spendable past full.

**Cost:** none in rework; it was caught in the design, by asking what the
losing condition actually reads.

**Generalisable:** "This costs something" is not the same as "this is
bounded". A cost only limits an action if the currency can run out *and* the
thing you are protecting depends on that currency. I had the right instinct —
price it — and had picked a currency with no floor, in a system where the
resource that matters is a different one entirely. Worth asking of any cost:
**what happens when the player has spent all of it?** If the answer is
"nothing changes", it was never a cost.

## Friction

Wiring the abilities into the live fight and then finding the path unreachable
from a test. `_spend_ability` fires when the pool empties *and* a repair
mechanism is available, and the mechanism needs a real terminal on both
streams — so a piped fight ends for lack of a repair pane rather than for lack
of repairs, and the ability correctly never gets a turn. That is the same gap
Q85 already names rather than a new one, and the placement is right for real
play, so the function is driven directly instead. Recorded rather than
contorted around.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Rationale | Reversible? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D213 | `Refactor` is priced in damage already dealt, not in boss health | Price it in HP conceded; a per-fight use cap; a third resource | M56. HP cannot end a fight and is unspendable past full, so it is not a bound. Progress is finite, and pricing in it makes the player who has achieved nothing exactly the one who cannot buy immunity. | Yes |
| D214 | The mutators live on `Fight`; the prices live on the abilities | Let abilities manipulate `Fight`'s private fields | The arithmetic that must stay consistent stays with the state it constrains, and every mutator clamps to what the fight can afford. | Yes |
| D215 | `use` reports the price actually paid | Report the price asked | Every mutator clamps, so the two can differ. An ability announcing a cost the fight could not meet would be lying on the screen. | Yes |
| D216 | `Steady Hand` needs two repairs before it may be used | Allow it at one | Spending your last repair to make a repair you can no longer afford free is a trap, not a choice. | Yes |
| D217 | `Overclock` empties the pool rather than reducing it | Leave one behind; convert a chosen number | A version that kept one is a free bonus. Emptying it makes the ability a decision about whether the fight is already over. | Yes |
| D218 | `--ability` follows `--fix`'s precedent for scripting a choice | An interactive menu in the repair pane | A menu is W11's territory once there is an earned loadout to choose from, and building one now would invent W11's gate early. | Yes |
