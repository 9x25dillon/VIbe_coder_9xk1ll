## What was built

### `fight.py` — two resources and nothing else

A boss starts on 100 HP. Per-step damages are **cumulatively rounded so they
sum to exactly 100**, which is not a detail: exit criterion 5 is an `== 0`, and
`total // steps` with the remainder dropped would leave a flawless fight
sitting on 1 HP and looking unwon.

Repairs are the other resource and they are deliberately *not* HP. Spending one
does two things:

- **halves what that step will deal**, compounding, so a second repair on one
  step leaves it worth a quarter; and
- **heals the boss**, by up to 10% of starting HP scaled by `1 - accuracy`.

The second is the mechanic. The direction is the same argument the Functional
axis rests on — **being close is rewarded**:

| What you repaired | Accuracy | Boss recovers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nearly right | 90% | 1 |
| Half working | 50% | 5 |
| A guess | 0% | 10 |

Over a five-repair pool that gap is roughly half the boss's health. A player
who ships something almost correct and patches it keeps nearly all their
damage; a player who repairs a guess watches the bar climb back.

```
  boss ████████████████████ 100   repairs ●●●●●
    ✔ cleared first try   -33
  boss █████████████░░░░░░░  67   repairs ●●●●●
    repaired — the boss recovers 8  (accuracy 17%)
    ✔ cleared after 1 repair   -17
  boss ████████████░░░░░░░░  58   repairs ●●●●·
    ✔ cleared first try   -33
  boss █████░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  25   repairs ●●●●·

  BOSS SURVIVES  on 25 hp; every step cleared, 1 repair spent
```

### Clearing and acing became different outcomes

`BOSS DOWN` is now reserved for HP actually reaching zero, which only a fight
with nothing spent can do. Everyone else clears the boss and leaves it
standing. That is the same distinction stars already draw for an ordinary
level, and both paths through `cmd_boss` share `_finish` so the live and plain
fights cannot drift into disagreeing about what winning means.

This is a **change to what the previous session shipped**, and it is worth
being explicit about. S019's CLI test asserted `BOSS DOWN` after a scripted
repair. That assertion is now wrong on purpose: the fight still completes and
still exits 0, and the test asserts `BOSS SURVIVES` and `every step cleared`
instead, which is stronger evidence for criterion 3 than the old one was.

### Accuracy had to become real (Q72, closed)

The heal is scaled by accuracy, and a stepped run watches exactly one case —
so the mechanic would have been dead on arrival, always scaling by the same
binary. The fix is to stop asking the stepped run for a verdict it cannot
give: the CLI measures accuracy with an ordinary `run_code` against the step's
whole test set, in a process of its own, while the paused child sits
untouched.

That is also now what decides whether a step cleared, which closes Q72. A live
boss step is judged on the same cases the same step faces when scored
normally, instead of on the single case that happened to be traced.

### `--repairs`

A fight can start with a different pool, and `--repairs 0` makes the first
failure final. It exists because exhaustion was otherwise unreachable from the
CLI and therefore untestable — and because [T4](../docs/trajectories/T4-adaptive.md)
W10's abilities are going to want to change it anyway.

### Classes, attributes and abilities, folded into T4

Not a new trajectory. T4 is the only one that models a player over time, and
its third hazard already forbids the exact mistake a class system invites:
*"Confusing what you write with what you are good at ... merging them is the
most tempting and most wrong simplification available here."*

So the layers are kept apart by construction. **Class** comes from the Vibe
Vector — how you write, an identity, never a rating. **Attributes** are the
per-tag mastery T4 W1 already models — what you are good at, with the evidence
attached. **Abilities** act on a fight's resources and each one costs
something. Added as W8–W11 with exit criteria 6–8; criteria 1–5 are untouched,
because adding scope with new criteria is planning and re-cutting old ones to
fit is not (N7).

Abilities have somewhere to act *because* W6 landed first. A bounded pool with
a cost curve is a thing an ability can modify; before this session there was
nothing to modify, which is why the idea waited rather than being started when
it was raised.

### Against T3's exit criteria

- **1–4** — verified in S017–S019.
- **5 — boss HP reaches zero only when every step passes:** **met**, and
  slightly more strongly than asked. Zero requires clearing every step *and*
  spending nothing, because healing only ever raises HP and a repaired step
  deals less. The criterion constrains one direction and this satisfies it in
  that direction.
- **6 — fully playable from the CLI:** still open, and now down to one thing.
  The fight is playable and it costs something; it is not *scored* on the
  three axes. That is W7, and `BOSS_WEIGHTS` (40/30/30) has been defined and
  unused since T3 was written.

## Misconceptions and corrections

### M37 — I keep asserting counts I have not measured

**Believed:** Three times across two sessions I wrote an expected number from a
mental model and let the test tell me. The bar count in
`test_health_is_shown_after_every_step` was asserted as `1 + 3 + 1` on the
theory that there is a closing bar; there is not, so it is 4. Before that,
M34's step count after an edit. And the test-count bump in `README.md` this
session went in as `1001` when discovery finds `973`.

**Revealed by:** `AssertionError: 4 != 5`, then
`README.md says 1001 tests; discovery finds 973`.

**Corrected to:** The numbers are cheap to measure and I was not measuring
them. The bar test now says what it means — one bar before the fight and one
after each step, asserting the first reads 100 and the last reads 0 — which is
both correct and a better test than a total would have been.

**Cost:** ~6 minutes across three instances, and none of it dangerous. Worth
recording because it is a *pattern* rather than an incident: two of the three
were caught by tests that exist precisely to catch stale numbers
(`test_living_docs_quote_the_real_test_count` is in the suite for this), which
means the project is already carrying the cost of a habit I could just drop.

**The generalisable part:** an assertion on a count is a claim like any other,
and CLAUDE.md's rule about evidence does not stop applying because the claim
is small. Measure, then write it down.

## Friction

- The first version of "no repairs left" used `break`, which would have
  **hung**: it leaves the loop while the child is still blocked mid-run, and
  the `live.drain()` immediately after waits forever for a child nobody has
  released. Caught by re-reading the branch before running it, not by a test —
  and a test would not have said much, because a hang shows up as a suite that
  never finishes rather than as a failure. It aborts now, and
  `test_running_out_does_not_hang_on_the_paused_child` exists so the question
  is not re-asked by hand.
- Measuring accuracy on every failure event was wasteful before it was
  guarded: a scripted or piped run has no repair on offer and was still paying
  for a sandboxed verdict nothing read. Now computed only when a repair is
  actually available.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Rationale | Reversible? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D141 | A repair spends from a bounded pool, reduces that step's damage, **and** heals the boss | Repairs cost player HP with a real fail state; repairs only reduce damage; repairs only count | The pool is a resource an ability can act on (T4 W10); the heal is what makes it a decision rather than a counter. The user chose the bounded pool and then added the damage reduction and the heal, which supersedes the "everyone who finishes sees BOSS DOWN" property that option originally carried | yes |
| D142 | The heal scales with `1 - accuracy` | Scale with accuracy; a flat heal | Being close is rewarded, which is the same argument the Functional axis rests on. A flat heal would make repairing a guess and repairing a near-miss identical, which is the distinction the game exists to draw | yes |
| D143 | Per-step damage is cumulatively rounded to sum exactly | `total // steps` with a remainder on the last step | Criterion 5 is an `== 0`. A rounding crumb makes a won fight look unwon, and a correction applied afterwards is a place for that crumb to hide | no |
| D144 | `BOSS DOWN` means zero HP; clearing every step is `BOSS SURVIVES` | Congratulate every completed fight identically | Clearing and acing are different outcomes, exactly as stars already distinguish them for a level. Both `cmd_boss` paths share `_finish` so they cannot drift | yes |
| D145 | Accuracy is measured by a separate full-test run, not by the stepped one | Keep judging on the watched case; run every test through the stepper | The heal needs a real fraction and a stepped run has one case. Running the set in its own process leaves the paused child untouched, and closes Q72 as a side effect | yes |
| D146 | Running out of repairs aborts the child, then returns | `break` out of the loop | The child is blocked mid-run; draining one nobody released waits forever. See Friction | no |
| D147 | Classes/attributes/abilities fold into T4 rather than opening a trajectory | A new T8; build them now alongside W6 | T4 is the only trajectory modelling a player over time, and its third hazard already forbids merging habits with mastery, which is the mistake a class system invites. Opening a fourth in-flight trajectory would also have worsened a rule the board already violates | yes |
| D148 | Class from the Vibe Vector, attributes from mastery, never averaged | One "level" number combining both | The hazard is already written down; a class system is the most attractive way anyone will find to violate it. Exit criterion 8 makes it checkable | no |
