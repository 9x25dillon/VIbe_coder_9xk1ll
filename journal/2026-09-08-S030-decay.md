## What was built

**Decay, in [`mastery.py`](../vibecoder/mastery.py).** An estimate loses half
its force every `HALF_LIFE_DAYS` (21). A half-life rather than a linear ramp
for the same reason `pulse.py` uses one: there is no age at which evidence
becomes worthless, and a linear decay has to invent one.

### Two things decay, and the second is the point

The obvious half is that **the value drifts toward `UNSEEN`** — toward the
middle, in whichever direction it sits, because age makes a rating *unknown*
rather than *bad*. A tag at 0.9 decays down; a tag at 0.1 decays **up**.

The half that matters is that **the observation count erodes with it**, so a
stale estimate stops being `confident`.

That is what answers Q90, which I flagged at the end of W5 as needing a
decision. The question was whether a tag decaying back under `DRILL_BELOW`
should be drilled — re-assessment or nagging? It turns out not to need a rule.
Decaying only the value would leave a returning player looking **measured and
mediocre**; decaying confidence too leaves them **unmeasured**. Those are
different states and they want opposite responses — one is a reason to drill
someone, the other a reason to ask them. `confident` is already this model's
vocabulary for "we do not know yet", so staleness is expressed in it rather
than in a second mechanism bolted alongside.

Measured, on a tag last played at 0.2 with nine observations:

| Away | Confident | Drill | Difficulty from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| same day | yes | `algorithms` | mastery |
| 3 weeks | yes | `algorithms` | mastery |
| 6 weeks | **no** | none | **default** |
| 10 weeks | no | none | default |

Six weeks away and the game stops assuming. It does not drill you for having
been absent, and it does not hand you the hard variant you earned in August.

### Decay is a view

`Mastery.as_of(now)` returns a decayed copy; `Session.current_mastery()` is
the one call site. The stored profile is never rewritten by the passage of
time, so it stays a record of what was actually measured rather than one that
quietly rots on disk — which is the same instinct N6 applies to `data/`.

The exception is `observe`, which takes age off *before* applying the new
evidence and writes that back. At that moment the decay is not an
interpretation of an old measurement: a new one has superseded it. Without
this, a player returning after two months would have moved from the rating
they left behind — measured at 0.81 instead of the 0.66 they should get.

## Misconceptions and corrections

### M52 — I nearly shipped the half of decay that does nothing useful

**Believed:** That decay meant "pull the value toward the middle", full stop.
That is what the trajectory's own sentence describes — *mastery drifts back
toward the middle over weeks* — and it is what I set out to implement.

**Revealed by:** Q90, which I had written at the end of W5 and which forced
the question before any code existed. If a tag decays from 0.2 toward 0.5, it
spends weeks *below* `DRILL_BELOW` on the way — so a player who went away
would be drilled on their return, on evidence that had aged past the point of
meaning anything. Decaying the value alone does not fix that; it prolongs it,
because a strong player decaying down *into* the drill band gets drilled too.

**Corrected to:** Decay has to reach `confident`, not only `value`. A stale
estimate should make the player *unmeasured*, and the model already has a word
for that. With the observation count eroding at the same half-life, six weeks
away drops a tag out of confidence entirely and the policy falls back to the
standard variant — which is what "re-assess returning players" actually means,
as opposed to "give them a slightly different number".

**Cost:** none in rework — it was caught before the code was written, by a
question I had written down rather than resolved. That is the argument for
recording open questions at the moment you notice them: this one cost five
minutes as a note in S029 and would have cost a session as a bug report.

**Generalisable:** A spec sentence can be complete about *what* moves and
silent about *what that means downstream*. "Drifts back toward the middle" is
accurate and would have produced a worse game, because the trajectory phrased
the mechanism and I was responsible for the consequence. The check that
worked: asking what each *other* part of the system would do with the decayed
value, one at a time.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Rationale | Reversible? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D192 | Decay erodes the observation count as well as the value | Decay the value only, as the trajectory's sentence describes | Otherwise a returning player reads as measured-and-mediocre rather than unmeasured, and gets drilled for having been away. Confidence is already the model's word for "we do not know yet" (M52, Q90). | Yes |
| D193 | Decay is a read-time view, never written to the profile | Decay on load; decay on save; a periodic sweep | The stored profile should stay a record of what was measured rather than one that rots on disk. Writing on load would also reset the clock it is measured against. | Yes |
| D194 | `observe` decays *before* applying new evidence, and writes that back | Observe from the stored value and let the view handle it | At that moment the decay is not an interpretation — a new run has superseded the old reading. Without it a two-month absence still moves from the August rating. | Yes |
| D195 | A half-life of 21 days | 7 days (too eager — a fortnight off would erase you); 90 days (too slow to re-assess anyone) | Puts a 90% estimate at 70% after a month and out of confidence after six weeks, which is roughly the interval over which a person's Python actually changes. Unplaytested and labelled as such. | Yes |
| D196 | A missing, mangled or naive timestamp decays nothing | Treat an unparseable stamp as ancient | The profile is a file the player is invited to edit, and entries written before W1 have no stamp at all. Refusing to decay costs a re-assessment; guessing "ancient" silently erases a profile. | Yes |
