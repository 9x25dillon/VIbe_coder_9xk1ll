## What was built

**Drills, in [`policy.py`](../vibecoder/policy.py).** A drill is
`DRILL_LENGTH` runs on the one tag the player is measurably weakest at. It
lives beside `choose_difficulty` because it is the same kind of decision —
*what should the game give you, and why* — and shares the rule that the reason
travels with the answer.

Three constraints shape it, and each rules something out rather than
decorating it:

- **Only a confident tag.** Drilling a tag measured once acts on a single
  unlucky afternoon, which is what `MIN_OBSERVATIONS` exists to prevent. A
  player who has never met a tag needs to *meet* it, and that is the selection
  ordering's job rather than a drill's.
- **Only below `DRILL_BELOW`.** A drill offered to someone who does not need
  one is noise, and it teaches them to ignore the next one.
- **`DRILL_LENGTH` is `MIN_OBSERVATIONS`**, not a round number picked by feel.
  After a drill the tag is `confident` again *by definition*, so the game is
  never adapting to a drill whose result it cannot yet measure. That is the
  one number here I did not have to guess.

`choose_drill` returns `None` — the common case — rather than an empty drill,
because "no drill" is an ordinary answer the caller phrases differently, not a
degenerate queue it can render the same way.

### Injected, not offered

The design says *drill injection*, and the word matters. A separate
`vibecoder drill` command would be a thing nobody runs. The moment after a
clear is when the game has the player's attention and is being asked "what
now", so that is where it goes — with campaign order still shown beneath it:

```
    [DRILL] algorithms is your weakest measured tag at 22%, so here are 3 runs on it
      1. w2-l3-join
      2. w3-l1-flatten
      3. w3-l2-window

    next up  Group and Total   w2-l2-groupby
```

Where several levels carry the tag the queue cycles them, because asking the
same question three times drills the *level* rather than the skill. Where one
does, it repeats — each run still draws a new seed and a fresh difficulty —
and the evidence reports `distinct_levels: 1` so the thinness is visible
rather than implied.

## Misconceptions and corrections

### M51 — I wrote Q88 down as a fact and it was not one

**Believed:** That three tags — `recursion`, `regex`, `numeric` — are carried
by exactly one level each and therefore "can never reach the confidence
threshold from ordinary play". I wrote that in S027's open questions, repeated
it in S028's, put it on the flight board, and told the user it was one of two
things that would become their call.

**Revealed by:** Checking it before building W5 on top of it, because W5 was
supposed to be its answer. Replaying one level three times banks three
observations — `submit` observes on every ranked run, not once per level — so
`recursion` becomes confident on the third replay. Sixteen lines of script.

**Corrected to:** The real limitation is narrower and different in kind. Those
tags *can* become confident; what they cannot do is be measured across more
than one problem shape, so the estimate reads "are you good at
`w3-l1-flatten`" more than "are you good at recursion". That is worth knowing
and worth stating, and it is not "permanently unusable for selection", which
is what I said. Q88 is rewritten.

**Cost:** ~5 minutes to check. The cost of *not* checking would have been W5
built to solve a problem that did not exist — and a drill's whole premise is
picking a confident tag, so I would have been designing around an impossibility
I invented.

**Generalisable:** This is the third session running where the finding came
from checking a claim rather than writing code, and the first where the claim
was **mine**. M43 was a criterion nobody had checked; M45 was a board summary I
quoted instead of the criteria; this is a sentence I authored, propagated
through three documents, and never tested. A claim gets more durable each time
it is repeated, and none of those repetitions is evidence. **The moment to
check a claim is when you are about to build on it** — which is also the
moment you are least inclined to, because it is load-bearing and checking it
might cost you the plan.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Rationale | Reversible? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D187 | A drill targets only a *confident* tag | Drill the weakest tag whatever the evidence | Drilling on one observation acts on an unlucky afternoon. A never-met tag needs meeting, which is the ordering's job. | Yes |
| D188 | `DRILL_LENGTH` equals `MIN_OBSERVATIONS` | Pick a round number like 3 or 5 independently | Tying them means the tag is confident again exactly when the drill ends, so the game never adapts to a result it cannot yet measure. The one number here that is derived rather than guessed. | Yes |
| D189 | Drills are injected after a clear, not behind a command | A `vibecoder drill` command; a prompt at level start | A command is a thing nobody runs. After a clear is when the player is already asking "what now", which is what the design means by *injection*. | Yes |
| D190 | A multi-level tag cycles its levels; a single-level tag repeats and says so | Always repeat the first matching level | Three runs of one level drills the level, not the skill. Where the content cannot offer variety, `distinct_levels` makes the thinness visible instead of implying a breadth that is not there. | Yes |
| D191 | `choose_drill` returns `None` rather than an empty drill | An always-present drill with a possibly-empty queue | "No drill" is the common case and the caller has to phrase it differently anyway. An empty queue is a shape that invites being rendered as if it were a drill. | Yes |
